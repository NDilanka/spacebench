# SpaceBench brand kit: gap analysis

Date: 2026-11-18. Status: analysis and proposals only. Nothing in the kit has been changed.

Method: all 137 reference images were viewed again one by one at full size (a few exact duplicates skipped), with written notes per image. The kit's 13 book sections and 17 exports were then viewed with the same eye and measured. Working notes are in `.work/gap_refs_01.md` to `gap_refs_20.md` and `.work/gap_kit.md`.

## 1. Verdict

The kit copied the skeleton of the references and left out what gives them character. It took the grid, the mono labels, the brackets, the hairlines, the index numerals and the dot numerals. It left out the subject, the light, the scale, the wit and the objects.

The book is a tidy design system. A reader can finish all 14 sections without seeing what a docking looks like. There is no capsule, no station, no Earth, no pilot and no flight shown as an image.

The plainest brand in the reference set (Shadcncraft: Geist, black and white, small geometric mark, tidy pages) is the one our kit resembles most. Even that brand has a photographed cover, a beam of light behind its interface and dense line figures. Ours has none of the three.

## 2. Five things in the kit that are too close to a reference

These are the most serious findings, because character cannot be borrowed.

| Kit element | Too close to | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cover line "Nothing invented. Everything measured." | Ontype, Proof No. 03: "Nothing Invented. Everything Derived." | Same construction, one word changed. A second reference uses "Nothing is skipped. Nothing is added without purpose." The whole pattern is used up. |
| Limb (particle arc), the kit's only key visual | Wantex | Wantex uses a particle arc with a dense spine and scattering grains on three separate images. Ours is the same picture. |
| Flight signature (strip of vertical bars) | Quattro 00/12 and Amatista | Both use a full-width bar strip. Amatista also marks a few bars in colour as the events that count. |
| Approach arcs | Ontype "One point." | Five arcs from one origin. Ours ends at one point instead of starting from one, which is not enough of a difference. |
| Landing identity (orange, dot numerals, grotesk on grey and ink, particle arc) | Wantex | Same four ingredients in the same roles. |

## 3. The gaps, with measurements

### 3.1 No subject, no key visual
- Kit: the vehicle appears as a thruster ring at about 280 px on a 1080 x 1350 poster and a docking ring at 230 px in the book. Nothing else.
- References: the brand's subject is shown many ways. DroneOS shows its drone as a photo, an x-ray wireframe, a particle cloud and a close-up. Physalia shows its jellyfish four times at four scales in three colour treatments. Achievable builds an isometric city that bleeds off three edges.

### 3.2 Flat surface, no light
- Kit: every piece is clean vector on a flat fill. Between 90.3% and 95.1% of every export is untouched ground (measured: marked area is 4.9% to 9.7%).
- References: about 60% carry a photograph or rendered light with grain. Recurring treatments: film grain and paper tooth, coarse visible halftone or line screen, ordered dither that dissolves a photo into the ground, blur as depth, one diagonal of light with a cold rim, and blue-black rather than neutral black. Monoic builds its whole identity on light with one hot line.

### 3.3 Accent is absent in practice
- Kit, measured: violet covers 0.05% to 0.42% of the area of the Dock exports. It reads as an interface state colour.
- References: accent is a full ground at least once in Achievable, Physalia, Meridian, Ventrix, Mentorfy, VAULT, Pem and Dzeinq. Several run three equal grounds: paper, ink and accent. Other uses we never make: a highlight block behind one word (Meridian "Field [Grade]"), a strip of tape over the tolerance zone (Quattro 02/12), one solid part in an exploded drawing (Pem), the whole drawing in accent on ink (Pem), and accent as light through a dot screen (Dzeinq).

### 3.4 One composition, timid scale
- Kit: all 17 exports use header row, title top left, figure, table, logo bottom right. The Layout section prescribes this ("each sheet has a header row, an index numeral, one statement and one figure"), so the standard itself causes the sameness. The mark is about 34 px in a 1200 px card, under 3% of the width. Largest type is roughly 30% of the width.
- References, measured: Quattro's headline word is 75% of the sheet width and its numeral is 19% of the height. Ontype's wordmark runs at 200% of the sheet width, cropped to four letters. Meridian's wordmark is cut in half by the bottom edge. Drawings are cropped hard to imply a bigger object. Titles are rotated onto a spine. Label stacks hang over a plate's corner. Cards overlap and run off the board. Posters are shown as objects on a wall with a tab stuck over the edge.

### 3.5 The mark has a good idea and a weak form
- The idea is the kit's best asset: a surveyor's bench mark, with a datum bar and a broad arrow.
- The form does not deliver it. A real bench mark is a bar with three cut strokes meeting at a point beneath it. Ours has two fat legs that do not meet, so the parent reads as an "upload" icon and the Dock mark as a "skip to end" button.
- The story is told in four lines of grey text and never shown: no stone, no survey, no docking plane.
- The system is rotation only. Arkade Studio builds letters and a four-way emblem from one arrow. Shadcncraft uses the mark's angle to notch the layout. Ontype crops its mark to giant tone-on-tone arcs. We do none of this.

### 3.6 The wordmark is a font, not a wordmark
- Kit: Geist 600 in two tones. Enlarged, it is still just Geist.
- References: Monoic (notched joints, chamfered corners), VAULT (the A is an arrow), Recove (mark and letters share one slit), Novum (the mark's star sits between the two words), Hypha (one custom swash, and a brand glyph swapped into headline letters), Wantex, Dzeinq, Meridian (weight 800, used very large). Monoic's wordmark is interesting enough to be the illustration when cropped at 210 px.

### 3.7 SB Matrix fails where it is used most
- The dot is 90% of the pitch, which is a sound ratio (Wantex uses about 85%). The problem is size. At 20 px type one dot pitch is about 2 px, and at 44 px about 4 px, so dots merge unevenly. The leaderboard's headline number, the success rate, is the least readable item in the table.
- The zero has an inner diagonal, so "0.08" reads close to "8.88" at display size.
- The face is never used as a giant readout, which is where a dot face looks best.
- The 22 pictograms are generic stroke icons. Quattro draws its icons on the same bitmap grid as its numerals.
- The "Display" row in the Typography section is clipped by the sheet edge. This is a layout bug.

### 3.8 The name is never explained, and half of it is unused
- Kit: nothing on the name. The voice is "mission control" in general.
- References: Monoic gives a formula ("Mono + IC"). Ontype gives dictionary entries for both halves of its name and a pun ("Every brand has a type. We set it."). Quattro and Meridian tell an origin story with a place, a year and a signature.
- "Bench" has three true meanings we never use: the test bench, the surveyor's bench mark, and the bench where judges sit (or where a player sits when benched).

### 3.9 Voice is correct and humourless
- Kit: a do and don't table. All six principles are prohibitions. The one line with wit is hidden in the Motion section: "An overshoot at a docking port is a failure."
- References: deadpan spec tables (Quattro: "Failure mode: Client requests 'something here'"; Achievable: "Raised by: People only"), footnotes that talk about the page ("* Every mark on this page has a reason."), headlines that state a surprising true fact (Physalia: "It looks like a jellyfish. It isn't one animal at all."), a diagram that narrates one incident with a number ("Nobody routed it.", 27 ms), fill-in-the-blank sentences, similes ("Cold start: under a heartbeat.").

### 3.10 No series, no provenance
- Kit: section numbers only.
- References: Ontype "Proof No. 01 to 07", Quattro "00/12 to 04/12", Meridian "Plate 04, Series A / Run 4 / Unit 118", Amatista "Opus 001", Pem "Fig. 01". The numbering is a brand property in itself.

### 3.11 No people
- Kit: none.
- References: people or hands appear in more than twelve images. A tiny figure for scale appears four times (Monoic, aura, Quattro, the R-mark beach). Two hands about to touch appear in Wantex; a handshake in Otherside and Evermist. The benchmark has human pilots as a first-class entity with two public boards, and the kit never shows one.

### 3.12 Nothing physical
- Kit: none.
- References: lanyard drawn in the brand's own line style, letterpress cards, hang-tag, tote, signage with reflections, stationery with visible grid, poster on a wall. Space culture offers more than any of these brands had to work with: mission patches, "remove before flight" tags, crew badges, cue cards, flight plans.

### 3.13 No motion in a kit about motion
- Kit: three dots on three tracks. Every graphic is static and sharp.
- References: VAULT's long-exposure car and smeared portrait, Physalia's 30 offset repeats of one curve, a waveform rendered as a lit ribbon.

### 3.14 Neutral temperature
- Kit: dead-neutral warm grey paper and ink.
- References: Ontype's sage and slate, Rendex's cream and warm black, Meridian's fog, the blue-black of every "logo on atmosphere" slide. A tint gives a brand a temperature before anything is drawn.

## 4. What is worth keeping

- The bench mark idea, the orientation system, and the per-simulation accent rule.
- The pilot display component and the label strip.
- The honesty devices: the SPECIMEN stamp, placeholder model names, the Wilson interval with ring terminals, the source line under the pass rule.
- The tokens, the build scripts and the theme switching.
- "Contact." at 120 px, which is the strongest thing in the book and appears once.

## 5. Proposal: make it the Bench

One idea ties the fixes together. SpaceBench stops presenting itself as a generic mission-control interface and becomes **a survey office for spaceflight**: it cuts marks, issues numbered plates and keeps specimens. Everything below follows from the name and from facts already in `DESIGN.md`. Nothing here is approved or built.

### 5.1 The mark, shown as what it is
- Refine the form toward a real cut bench mark: bar above, three strokes meeting at a point. Keep the 8 x 8 module grid, the gap and the orientation system.
- Add cut versions: chiselled (two-tone V-cut), stencilled, outline, dot.
- Every scored flight gets a **bracket number** in the manner of a surveyor's flush bracket, for example `SB-D4-0017`, shown as a small riveted plate. This becomes the provenance device on every artefact, together with the state hash, which this project really computes at every tick.
- Compound emblems from the same arrow: four around one datum make a docking reticle, three at 120 degrees make the three petals, twelve around a ring make the twelve hooks.
- Use it large: cropped tone-on-tone at ten times size, as a notch in a panel edge, as the separator between "Space" and "Bench".

### 5.2 A wordmark with one cut
Give the letters one detail taken from the mark, so the name is worth cropping at poster size. Candidates to test: a stencil bridge the width of the mark's gap; the bar of the mark replacing the crossbar of the "A"; a heavier weight. Stays grotesk, as decided.

### 5.3 Five images that could only be ours
All are drawn from flight data or from one geometry file, so they need no licence and stay true.
1. **Bullseye trace.** The pilot's view down the docking axis: concentric rings at the real limits with the 0.10 m circle in the centre, and the trail of positions converging on it. Where the trail ends is the result. Thirty seeds overlaid make a rosette per model and tier, a fingerprint. This replaces the approach arcs.
2. **Stroboscopic approach.** The capsule drawn at every decision step, like a multiple-exposure photograph. Gaps are coasts, clusters are hesitation. It shows the pulse-and-coast action model at a glance and puts motion into a still.
3. **Thruster score.** Sixteen rows, one per thruster, time running left to right, pulses with real length. It replaces the bar strip. Sixteen rows belong to us; a barcode belongs to anyone.
4. **The sensor's view.** The station as a lidar point cloud whose noise grows with range, using the sourced figures (6 mm sideways and 34 mm along the line of sight at 10 m). Sharp where measured, soft where not. The grain is data, not decoration.
5. **Orbital sunrise.** A procedurally drawn Earth limb at the terminator: a thin cold rim on blue-black with grain. The mark's datum bar sits exactly on the limb. This replaces the particle arc and gives the cover real light.

### 5.4 The vehicle and the port, drawn properly
- One geometry file produces every view, so they agree: plan, profile, section, sector, elevation, an exploded isometric of the port with exactly one violet part ("Fig. 01, The Port"), a wireframe and a point cloud.
- **Approach plates.** Aviation approach plates combine a plan view, a profile view and a table of minima. Docking has all three: the corridor, range against time with holds at 250, 30 and 10 m, and the contact limits. One numbered plate per tier (D-1 to D-6) replaces the tier posters.
- Scale contrast as the emotional core: a 4 m capsule, a very large station, a 0.10 m tolerance. A tiny capsule against a hard-cropped station is our version of the lone figure on the beach.
- Any dimension on a drawing must be checked against the IDSS document first. None will be invented.

### 5.5 Violet with real presence
- Three equal grounds for brand pieces: paper, ink and violet. One full violet poster paired with a quiet twin.
- A violet tape band laid over the tolerance zone of a chart. Pass means the trace ends inside the tape.
- A highlight block behind one number in a headline: "Dock at [0.10 m/s]."
- Violet as light on ink: thruster plume glow rendered through the SB Matrix dot screen.
- The interface stays restrained. The one-accent-per-simulation rule holds.

### 5.6 Composition rules that allow character
Replace the single sheet anatomy with six or more named compositions: spine title, cropped giant, plate with hanging label stack, slab overlapping two layers, device as headline frame (the pilot display, cropped by the edge), and deliberate emptiness. Add rules for crop, bleed, overlap and scale contrast.

### 5.7 Surface and temperature
- Grain, a coarse line screen, a dither dissolve and blur for depth, each defined as a token with fixed settings.
- Tint the neutrals: a cool clean-room white for paper and a blue-black for ink.

### 5.8 Voice with a pulse
- A dictionary entry for the name. "bench, n. 1 a table where things are tested. 2 (surveying) bench mark: a cut in stone that fixes a known height. 3 where the judges sit."
- The origin story is real and strange: in a probe of 1,000,000 inputs, the native `atan2` disagreed with `libm` 125,801 times. "Founded on a disagreement about atan2."
- Headlines that state a true surprising fact: "The simulator stops while the pilot thinks." "Docking is a slow collision." "Sixteen thrusters. None of them points where you want."
- Similes that check out: the minute hand of a clock turns at 0.1 deg/s, so the 0.20 deg/s limit is "roll no faster than two minute hands".
- Deadpan spec rows and one self-referential footnote: "* Every number on this page came from a seeded run. This one is a specimen."
- One-word posters for the four verbs of a flight: Hold. Coast. Pulse. Contact.
- A result sentence as a format: "Model [ A ] reached [ 30 m ] and then [ ran out of fuel ]."
- Tagline candidates on our own name, to replace the cover line: "Every model gets the same bench." "Benched, or docked." "Marked at the bench."

### 5.9 People
- The human pilot: a hand on a hand controller, a figure at a console seen from behind, drawn in the kit's line and dither language.
- "The last ten centimetres": probe and port about to touch, labelled with the real limit.

### 5.10 Objects
Mission patch per simulation, bracket plate, "remove before flight" tag, numbered crew badge, cue card, printed approach plate, sticker sheet. Drawn in the kit's own line style, as Ontype draws its lanyard, so no stock mockups are needed.

### 5.11 Type repairs
- SB Matrix: a minimum size of about 80 px (8 px pitch), with Geist Mono or a pixel-snapped cut below that. Open the zero. Use it as a giant readout.
- Redraw the pictograms on the dot grid so numerals and icons are one family.
- A heavier display weight (700 to 800) at much larger sizes.
- Fix the clipped Display row.

### 5.12 Motion and sound
- Logo animation: the arrow closes on the bar, slows, and stops at the gap. No bounce.
- Replay scrubber as a logarithmic range ruler from 400 m to 0, with tall ticks at 250, 30 and 10 m, so the last 10 m get room.
- Readouts step rather than tween. A short thruster tick as the one sound.

### 5.13 Borrowed history, optional
Public-domain rendezvous and docking diagrams (Gemini 8 is already cited in the brief as the stuck-thruster precedent) and 19th-century surveying engravings, printed in one ink. Each image needs a licence check, attribution, and no agency insignia.

## 6. Open points that need a decision

1. Whether to rebuild in one go or to make a small proof round first.
2. Whether the mark's form may be refined (the chosen candidate A stays; its strokes change).
3. Whether imagery is generated only, or may also include public-domain photographs and engravings.
4. Whether the system gets one unruly element, and which one. Serif stays excluded.
5. Whether the neutrals may be tinted.

## 7. Facts to verify before use

- Station length and any docking-interface dimension (not in `DESIGN.md`).
- That Gemini 8 was the first docking (the brief cites it only as a thruster-fault precedent).
- Any simile other than the minute-hand one, which is arithmetic (360 degrees in 3600 s).
- The licence of every borrowed image.
