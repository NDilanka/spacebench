# Independent review of the proof round (Fable)

Date: 2026-11-18. Reviewer worked alone. All 137 references were viewed one by one at full size before any other analysis was read. Notes per image are in `.work/fable/refs_01.md` to `refs_23.md`. Notes on the nine proof pieces are in `.work/fable/proof_01.md` to `proof_03.md`. No kit file was changed.

Limits of this review: colours and sizes from references are read by eye from compressed JPEGs, so hex values are approximate. No byte-identical duplicates exist (MD5 checked); 15 near-duplicates were noted and skipped. The older kit in `brand/exports` was not re-reviewed. The proof pieces were viewed as rendered between 10:11 and 10:19 on 18 Nov.

## 1. Verdict

| Axis | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Aesthetic quality | 5 / 10 | The typesetting is competent, but every surface is flat colour under one noise overlay, several elements collide, and the stations and the pilot are placeholder drawings. |
| Nostalgia | 3 / 10 | Nostalgia exists only as format (an approach plate, a patch). No piece shows a print or drafting process: no ink behaviour, no screen, no paper, no real hand. |
| Remarkability | 4 / 10 | Only p04 passes the across-the-room test. The cover is the most common space image there is, and the Dock mark reads as a "skip to end" button. |
| Data-driven, advanced tech | 6 / 10 | The thruster score and the approach plate are real strengths. But the flights are procedural curves, the traces do not converge, and the sensor view ignores true scale. |

Overall: the proof round fixed the first kit's emptiness by adding nine directions at once. The best references do the opposite. Each one repeats one or two devices without mercy. SpaceBench still cannot be described in six words. The round is a clear step up, and it is about halfway to the best references.

## 2. What the references do that the proof round still does not

1. **One signature, repeated.** Every strong brand in the folder fits in six words. Monoic: one red light streak on grey grain (`HWsLgryX`, `HWsLiMIX`). Physalia: a jellyfish in 45-degree line halftone, one blue (`HVGemWUW`, `HU8l0KGW`). Meridian: a faded blue title block, fog, a half-cropped wordmark (`HTfc6bHW`, `HTfc7eoX`). Ontype: a brush wordmark stamped over tidy type (`HTK7mF8X`). The proof round has nine devices and no signature.
2. **A print process, not a noise overlay.** `HXB7l9pX` is a cyanotype: navy with heavy 1 to 2 px grain and a blurred white photogram, with square data nodes and value chips ("SOM 4.1%") on top. `HVbI-MfX` renders a tree as vertical lines at about 3 px pitch whose weight follows the image. `HMehAiIW0` prints wood engravings in one ultramarine ink at about 55% ink coverage. `HFkgP7rX` has coarse halftone at about 6 px pitch. The proof round uses the same `grain.png` at 11 to 55% opacity on every sheet.
3. **Light.** Monoic's accent is never flat. It is a hot streak with a white core and a soft halo, about 4% of the frame, pushed into a corner and cropped. `HCQSlv7b` shows a three-way seam of light on black. `G-P6cRjW` gets all its colour from halation through frosted glass. In the proof round violet is always a flat fill.
4. **Scale contrast with nothing in between.** Quattro `HS5xdH1W`: the headline is about 13% of the sheet height, the labels 1.5%, and there are only three sizes on the sheet. Ontype `HTK7m9MW`: the wordmark runs at about 250% of the sheet width, cropped to four letters, above 20 px text. The proof round uses five to seven sizes per sheet, and its largest elements compete in pairs (p03 title against "D-4", p06 headline against numerals).
5. **Charged emptiness.** Quattro `HS5xexRX` leaves 52% of the sheet empty and says so in the copy. Ontype `HTK7mF8X` is 58% empty. Meridian `HTfc8OCW` leaves the middle 45% blank. The emptiness works because one strong subject sits against it. p01, p05 and p08 are full boards with many items of equal weight.
6. **Real line-weight hierarchy in drawings.** Meridian `HTfc7eoX`: frame 1 px, subject 2 px, dimensions hairline, centre lines dash-dot and running past the frame into the next layer. Leader lines end in ringed dots. p03 draws almost everything at one weight and its labels float.
7. **A subject drawn many ways from one model.** DroneOS (`HROmPjTa`) shows one drone as a dark photo, a wireframe with perspective lines, a point cloud of about 1,500 points with a dense off-centre core and point sizes from 1 to 6 px, and a close-up with glass chips pinned to parts. The proof round has three different stations (a black block in p03, a hatched block in p05, an H shape in p07) and one tiny capsule.
8. **Accumulation.** `HPnv8y9WA` builds a globe from thousands of lines at about 15% opacity. The shape appears only where lines pile up. p04 draws 29 traces at one opacity, and they do not pile up anywhere.
9. **Objects drawn honestly.** Ontype draws its lanyards as pure line art (`HUyssnEW`). p08 says "drawn, not photographed" and then imitates photographs with drop shadows and CSS metal.
10. **People for scale, or anonymous and lit.** A tiny figure appears in `HRT0BKxX`, `HFkgP7rX` (UMBRAL, a person at the centre of giant rings), `HDSuE-0W` and `G-Ur3LCX`, at 2 to 5% of the frame height. A large figure appears only from behind as a silhouette against a bright scene (`HCWj_rfX`) or with the face hidden (`HCQSlv7b`). p09 has no light and no readable figure.
11. **A mark that sits on an edge of light.** `HD66vVFX`, `HIBHih_W`, `HCWj_rjW` and `HDSuE-0W` all place a small white mark, 9 to 19% of the width, on a lit diagonal or a horizon. p02 has the right idea and loses it in a soft glow.
12. **Type with a quirk.** Ontype and the Fig. cards (`HUxqfTaWE`) set labels in a pixel-like OCR mono. Monoic's wordmark has a chamfer and a notch worth enlarging to 36% of the frame height (`G-Ur5cOW`). The one brand that uses plain Geist (`HNh6CQGX`) is the dullest in the folder.

## 3. Piece by piece

### p01 Mark
- Works: the chiselled version is the first thing in the brand with material and light. The reading "bar is the docking plane, arrow is the approach, gap is the last distance" is true and good. The dictionary entry has charm. The emblems use real counts.
- Defects:
  - Rotated 90 degrees, the Dock mark is the media "skip to end" icon. Rotated 180 degrees, Landing is a "download" icon. The refinement did not fix this, because the cause is the silhouette (a triangle against a bar of the same width), not the stroke shape. At 24 and 16 px the three strokes merge and nothing is left to tell them apart.
  - W-A and W-B cut every closed counter. At lockup size the cuts look like font rendering faults, mostly in "a", "e" and "B". A and B are nearly the same.
  - The dot version turns the arrow into a blob.
  - The bracket plate is a CSS metal gradient with embossed text. It is the cheapest-looking object in the round.
  - The last lockup row is clipped by the bottom of the capture.
  - The pen notes are a font (Reenie Beanie). All three have the same size, slant and stroke, so they read as typeset.
  - The lower 40% of the left panel is empty noise.
  - The sheet is a specimen board with over 20 tiles of equal weight.
- Fixes: see improvements 6 and 7. Drop the dot version and the metal plate. Show the mark once, large, and move the trials to a second sheet.

### p02 Cover
- Works: horizon as datum. "Every model gets the same bench." is the best line in the round. The honest footer.
- Defects:
  - A blue sunrise over a planet limb is the stock image of the whole space industry. Remarkability is close to zero.
  - It is blue. `gen/limb.py` uses a halo of RGB (74, 104, 190) for the tint called "mono". The parent brand is monochrome and Dock is violet. This is a third hue.
  - The glow is a smooth airbrush. Wantex builds its limb from points (`HF4fY56a`). Novum uses one hard lit ridge (`HD66vVFX`).
  - The flat white arrow is pasted on a soft render. Nothing lights it. The "gap" named in the caption cannot be seen because the tip sits in the glare.
  - The caption keys (DATUM, GAP, ARROW) are at about 15% contrast.
  - The wordmark cuts read as dirt at 30 px.
- Fixes: retire the sunrise. Use improvement 1 (the circle) as the paper cover and improvement 4 (the hash weave) as the ink cover. If a horizon is kept, make it a single hard hairline that crosses the sheet (improvement 7), in neutral tones only.

### p03 Approach plate
- Works: the best piece for data and nostalgia together. The aviation plate format truly fits (holds, minima, "Missed approach: there is none"). Real limits, correct IDSS note, "NOT FOR NAVIGATION", the decision tick strip.
- Defects:
  - The station is a black rounded rectangle with the word STATION. It touches the frame.
  - The three-row label stack (white, violet, ink) at the lower right of the figure is taken directly from the Fig. cards (`HUxqfTbW`). It also sticks out about 13 px past the margin, which reads as a bug.
  - The pen note "rates under 0.2 before you go" is struck through by the violet event line.
  - The violet slab "FINAL 10 M" is rotated, overruns the frame, and hides the most important part of the curve under solid violet.
  - The dashed 200 m line in the profile has no label.
  - "D-4" and the title have the same weight. The stencil D reads as "I)".
  - One line weight for almost everything. No dimension lines. Labels float beside arcs.
  - Units are inconsistent: "deg/s" in the limit column, "°/s" in the flight column, and "1.6 °" with a space.
  - The trace has cusps on the hold arcs because it comes from a procedural curve in `proof.js`, not from a flight.
  - The paper is a flat web background. Nothing says "printed chart".
- Fixes: replace the label stack with a proper title block (plate number, tier, seed, tick count, state hash, revision). Use three line weights. Make the slab a translucent band behind the lines, square to the frame. Label the 200 m line. Print the plate with the process in improvement 3. Draw the port from the geometry file (improvement 5).

### p04 Bullseye
- Works: the one image you would recognise from across a room: violet sheet, black disc, white threads. The count is right (24 inside, 6 outside). The headline is good.
- Defects:
  - The traces are scribbles. They loop like hair. Nothing funnels toward the port. On the one poster that is meant to be a data portrait, the data reads as decoration. The cause is `lateralAt` in `proof.js`, which multiplies a spiral by sine terms.
  - The hero trace starts in a smeared blob of overlapping beads at the upper right.
  - The SPECIMEN tag is crossed by the hero trace, with a bead on the word RESULT. The footer text is crossed by two traces. The label that must be legible is not.
  - The 1 m and 10 m rings named in the footer cannot be seen.
  - "/30" uses a different dot pitch, a lower baseline and a dotted slash that reads as noise.
  - All end dots are one size.
- Fixes: improvements 1 and 2. Keep the composition. Replace the traces with real ones, drawn additively. Keep type and tags clear of the data with a mask.

### p05 Flight sheet
- Works: the thruster score is the most convincing data graphic in the round. Sixteen rows, 374 pulses, and the stuck thruster visible as a violet smear. The headline arithmetic is right (0.139 minus 0.10).
- Defects:
  - The violet box cuts off the bottoms of "Flight 0017 arrived". Visible clipping.
  - The strobe draws every frame at the same weight, so it becomes a ribbon of rectangles. It reads as a bent duct. Only the last frame looks like a capsule.
  - The violet ticks under the strobe are not explained in any caption.
  - "HOLD 250 M" is crossed by the keep-out line.
  - Three caption lines at 9.5 px and low contrast carry the whole explanation.
  - Four items of near-equal weight. The weakest (the strobe) has the centre.
  - A hatched block stands in for the station, and it differs from the one in p03.
- Fixes: give the score the centre and two thirds of the sheet. Replace the strobe with improvement 10. Enlarge the captions to 12 px at full contrast or cut them.

### p06 Slow collision
- Works: the best copy ("Docking is a slow collision." "Too gentle is also a fail."). The facts check out (10 m at 0.10 m/s is 100 s). The numerals carry across a room.
- Defects:
  - The grey band at the top and the pale rays are the mark at 1740 px, cropped. Nobody will see that. The band reads as an image that failed to load, and the rays as an unexplained fan.
  - The "g" of "Docking" drops into the violet box and the "ll" and "i" of "collision" poke into it from below. It reads as tight leading, not as a pun.
  - Headline and numerals compete.
  - The sheet combines Meridian's highlight box (`HTfc8OCW`), Wantex's dot numerals (`HF4faIVX`) and Quattro's deadpan spec rows (`HS5xfjcW`). Nothing on it is ours except the words.
- Fixes: delete the ghost mark and the violet box. Choose one giant: the numerals. Set the sentence small. Better: turn this fact into improvement 1, where the limit is shown at actual size.

### p07 Sensor view
- Works: the strongest concept in the round. "The pilot never sees the station" is true to the headline track, and the labels are honest. The noise at 10 m matches the source.
- Defects:
  - The point images are flat stipples with one dot size, two or three flat grey steps and hard rectangular outlines. They look like dithered clip art.
  - Every range fills its panel. A fixed 26-degree sensor would see a small station at 250 m and a wall at 10 m. Rescaling throws away both the truth and the drama.
  - "Soft where not": nothing is soft. A noise of 850 mm along the line of sight at 250 m should smear the image visibly.
  - The 10 m image is clipped by straight column edges. The three images share no centre line.
  - The stats are 9.5 px at low contrast.
- Fixes: improvement 8.

### p08 Objects
- Works: the content is right. The checklist values match the pass rule. "The four verbs of a flight" is ownable. The SPECIMEN streamer is a clever answer to having no results. The patch is the most nostalgic object in the round.
- Defects:
  - The caption says "drawn, not photographed". The rendering imitates photographs: drop shadows, small rotations, gradient metal. It is the look of a generic mock-up pack.
  - The stickers are four rounded rectangles in Geist ExtraBold with a full stop. Every software company has these.
  - The patch has an icon in a ring where a mission patch has a picture. The petals emblem resembles a radiation trefoil. The lower arc text is too small to read.
  - The streamer does not have "remove before flight" proportions, and its grommet looks like a button.
  - The lanyard repeats the Dock mark, which reads as a column of skip buttons.
  - Captions float far from their objects. Violet is scattered over about 15% of the sheet.
- Fixes: draw every object as flat line art with one fill. Show three objects, not seven, one of them large and cropped. Give the patch a scene: the port ring, the approach line, the datum.

### p09 Pilot
- Works: the idea matches the references (figure from behind, anonymous). The copy is exact.
- Defects:
  - The figure does not read as a person. `gen/pilot.py` draws shoulders, a seat, an arm and a hand controller, but only the rim is lit, so the render shows a black keyhole with a white outline on a stalk. The hand is an unidentifiable blob at the lower right.
  - The display shows white bars for text. The brand has a real pilot display. "Same display" is the claim, so the display must be the real one.
  - Ordered dither on flat vector shapes is only a pattern fill. Dither earns its place on continuous tone (`HRT0BKxX`, `HHFS_j1a`).
  - The headline touches the monitor edge. Stray dither rows spill above the monitor and at the lower corners.
  - The violet disc with a swirl has no clear meaning.
- Fixes: improvement 12. This piece should not go to the owner in its current state.

### Across all nine
- Seven of nine use one headline formula: two or three lines of Geist ExtraBold, tight tracking, one line in slate or one word in a violet box.
- Violet behaves seven ways: field, highlight box, slab, line, ring, disc, scattered detail.
- Dot numerals clog at small sizes (p04 "/30", p07, p08).
- Essential explanations sit in 9.5 px mono at low contrast.

## 5. Where the gap analysis is wrong, shallow or missed something

The gap analysis is accurate on most points: no subject, no light, timid accent, one composition, weak mark form, no series, no people, no objects. I agree with its "keep" list. These are its faults.

1. **It reads emptiness as a defect.** It reports 90 to 95% untouched ground as a problem. The references are 45 to 85% empty on purpose. The real fault was that nothing charged the space. The proof round then over-filled p01, p05 and p08.
2. **It swaps one look-alike for a cliché.** It flags the particle limb as Wantex and proposes a sunrise limb instead. That is still a limb, it is the most common space picture, and as built it is blue.
3. **It misdiagnoses the mark.** It blames two fat legs. The refined mark has three clean strokes and still reads as a skip button at 90 degrees. The cause is the silhouette under rotation.
4. **It never asks for one signature.** It lists thirteen directions. The proof round built nine at about 60% each. The references win by depth.
5. **It treats nostalgia as a surface token.** "Grain, line screen, dither, blur, each a token." Nostalgia in the references comes from a whole process: cyanotype, engraving, letterpress, a drafted plate with a title block. It missed `HXB7l9pX`, which is the clearest example of nostalgia and data in one image.
6. **It did not notice that real data now exists.** `crates/core` runs, the hash gate is green, and `ORDERS.md` records a negative control in which native and WebAssembly builds first differ at tick 44, 23 and 1. The proof round still draws procedural curves. The brand rule "every graphic is derived from simulator data" is met in name only.
7. **It left the unruly element as an open question.** The proof round answered with a handwriting font. A font is the opposite of unruly.
8. **People.** It counted them correctly and then proposed a pilot at a console. The references use people for scale or as lit silhouettes.
9. **Dot ratio.** It calls a dot of 90% of the pitch sound and says Wantex uses about 85%. In `HF4faIVX` the large "W/04" looks much airier to me, nearer 50 to 70%. I could not measure this exactly from the JPEG. Test 70% before fixing the ratio.
10. **Type.** It notes that the plainest reference brand uses Geist and stops there. With Geist and Geist Mono fixed by decision, the character has to come from the lettering that is now allowed (stencil, engraved, hand). The proof round uses each of those once, as a trial. They need to become a system.

## 6. The twelve highest-impact improvements, ranked

### 1. The Circle, at actual size
- Lifts: remarkability, nostalgia.
- Make: a poster at A2 portrait (420 x 594 mm) with one ink disc exactly 200 mm across. A dash-dot crosshair runs through it to all four sheet edges. One line of 5 mm caps: "ACTUAL SIZE. SCALE 1:1." One sentence inside the disc in paper colour, small: "From 400 m away, arrive inside this circle." Bottom left, three drawn measures: a 100 mm bar labelled "one second at the fastest speed allowed, 0.10 m/s", a 50 mm bar for 0.05 m/s, and a wedge of exactly 4.0 degrees. One more true line: "The capsule is 4 m wide. That is twenty of these." Then a Dock twin on a full violet field, with the thirty end points plotted at actual size once real flights exist. The ink disc becomes the brand's recurring shape: favicon fallback, sticker, patch centre, the full stop after a headline.
- Evidence: p04's disc is the only memorable shape in the round. Fig. cards print "SCALE 1:1 · DO NOT SCALE DRAWING" (`HUxqfTaXA`). Quattro makes a tolerance the headline (`HS5xexRX`, "±0.05"). UMBRAL puts a figure at the centre of rings (`HFkgP7rX`).
- True because: sideways misalignment at first contact is limited to 0.10 m, so the allowed zone is a circle of 0.10 m radius. p04 already treats it this way. Confirm with the owner that the limit is a radial offset. The 4 m width is the family-level figure in `DESIGN.md` section 11, so label it Dragon-class. On screens, state the print size instead of claiming 1:1.

### 2. Real flights from the real core
- Lifts: data, aesthetic.
- Make: replace `SBP.flight` with trajectories exported from `crates/core`. Until the classical controller of build step 2 exists, fly scripted burns and label them "SCRIPTED BURN · NOT A PILOT". After that, use the PID or LQR baseline and label it "REFERENCE CONTROLLER". That is real simulator data and it is not a model result, so it needs no SPECIMEN tag. Redraw p04 with these traces at 6 to 10% white, drawn additively, so the funnel appears where they pile up. Add a second figure: range on a log axis against closing speed for thirty flights. Speed falls to zero at 250, 30 and 10 m, so the figure has three teeth. The contact window from 0.05 to 0.10 m/s is a band at range zero. Label four nodes on it: three holds and contact.
- Evidence: additive line density in `HPnv8y9WA`. Labelled nodes on a generative figure in `HIBHekyW`. A path that narrates one event with a number in `HVGenCaW`.
- True because: the brand rule says every graphic is derived from simulator data. `ORDERS.md` shows the core and the gate are working. I did not check whether the core can already export a trajectory. If it cannot, that is a small order for the lead.

### 3. A print process for Dock: the violet line
- Lifts: nostalgia, aesthetic, remarkability.
- Make: print every Dock plate in one ink. Violet `#6B3BFF` for all lines and all type, on a warm white paper (a tinted neutral, for example near `#F2EFE8`). No black on the sheet. Define the process once, as code: lines thicken by about 0.3 px and are thresholded so that ink pools at corners and breaks on hairlines; exposure is uneven by a few percent across the sheet; two fold lines at the thirds; punched holes on the left edge; a ruled title block with plate number, tier, tick count, state hash and revision letter. Apply it to p03 first. Then fix violet to three behaviours only: ink on paper, light on ink, and one full field per series. Remove highlight boxes and slabs.
- Evidence: one-ink printing in `HMehAiIW0`. Cyanotype with data nodes in `HXB7l9pX`. Meridian's title block over a dimensioned drawing in `HTfc6bHW`. Monoic's accent as light in `HWsLgryX`.
- True because: engineering drawings were long reproduced as diazo whiteprints with blue to violet lines on white, and spirit duplicators printed in purple. This is from general knowledge. Verify it before using it in copy. The look stands without the claim. Landing can take the same process in its orange.

### 4. The hash weave, and the two builds as mirror twins
- Lifts: data, remarkability.
- Make: the core hashes the whole state at every tick. Draw each 64-bit hash as a column of 64 cells, one column per tick. A thousand ticks make a band of 1000 by 64 cells. Place the native band above a datum hairline and the WebAssembly band mirrored below it. When the builds agree, the figure is perfectly symmetric. Then show the negative control from `ORDERS.md`: for seed 1 the symmetry breaks at tick 44, for seed 2 at tick 23. Headline: "Same bits." A short strip of this weave replaces every decorative barcode and sits in each title block as the flight's signature. This is a parent-brand piece in ink and paper only, and it should be the ink cover.
- Evidence: Quattro's modulated barcode (`HS5xexRX`). Amatista's bars with raised events (`HMzw7EkW`). Bitacora's mirrored ornament built from one unit (`HWdESPhX`). The pixel-stretch bands in `HXB7oGtX`.
- True because: this is build step 1's own gate. Not verified: whether `native_hash` and `wasm_hash.mjs` can print a hash for every tick. The recorded values are for ticks 0, 1, 7, 1000 and 200000 only. Do not draw the weave from invented bits.

### 5. One geometry file, one vehicle, a series of Fig. plates
- Lifts: nostalgia, data, aesthetic.
- Make: one geometry file for the capsule and the port. Every view comes from it, so the three different stations go away. Fig. 01, The Port: an exploded isometric with three petals and twelve hooks, one petal in solid violet. Fig. 02, The Sixteen: the capsule as a wireframe with sixteen force arrows of equal length (400 N each) and their torque arms; small chips pinned to parts carry real values ("400 N", "0.035 s minimum pulse", "x16"). Fig. 03, The Corridor: 400 m to contact on the log ruler, the 200 m sphere, the three holds. Shade solids with engraving-style hatching whose spacing follows the surface angle to one light. Three line weights. Centre lines run past the frame.
- Evidence: exploded view with one solid accent part in `HUxqfTaXA`. Chips pinned on parts in `HPnv8y7Xk`. Wireframe in `HROmPjTa`. Engraving line in `HMehAiIW0`. Meridian line weights in `HTfc7eoX`.
- True because: 16 thrusters at 400 N, 12 hooks, 3 petals and the 0.035 s pulse are in the brief. The layout is not, so every plate carries "SCHEMATIC · DRAGON-CLASS FROM PUBLIC PARAMETERS". Print no dimension that is not in `DESIGN.md` or the IDSS document.

### 6. Two human traces: the stamp and the pen
- Lifts: remarkability, nostalgia.
- Make: the SPECIMEN tag becomes a rubber stamp. Stencil caps, 12 to 18% of the sheet width, rotated 4 to 8 degrees, printed over the typography and allowed to cover part of it. Model the ink in code: uneven pressure, dropouts, a heavier edge. Seed it with the bracket number so no two impressions match. Later states use the same stamp: "RE-SIMULATED" and "REFEREE SIGNED" with the first eight hash characters. For the pen, drop Reenie Beanie. Either the owner writes the notes once on paper and they are traced, or the notes are drawn in a single-stroke plotter alphabet with pen lifts and slight overshoot. Pen notes are few, large, and allowed to cross a frame, but never a label.
- Evidence: Ontype stamps its brush wordmark over a line of text (`HTK7mF8X`, `HQr7Y8vX`). The faint signature in `HFzztKCa`.
- True because: SPECIMEN is mandatory until results exist, the server re-simulates submitted flights, and the referee signs telemetry. The stamp turns three rules into the brand's one rude element. A single-stroke plotter face such as the Hershey set is an option; check its licence first.

### 7. The long datum
- Lifts: remarkability, aesthetic.
- Make: stop treating the bar of the mark as a short bar. On every sheet one hairline crosses from edge to edge. The mark's bar is a thick segment of that line and the arrow points at it. Parent: horizontal, arrow below. Dock: vertical, one eighth in from the right edge; the port and station live beyond it and the approach comes from the left. Landing: horizontal, one eighth up from the bottom. This keeps the orientation system and turns it into a layout you can identify from a distance. For the standalone mark, extend the bar about 15% past the arrow on each side and narrow the strokes so the gaps are wider than the strokes. Below 24 px use the upright parent mark only, with the simulation shown by the accent. For the wordmark, drop the counter cuts at text sizes. Use stencil caps only at display size, for codes ("D-4"), the stamp and poster-scale "SPACEBENCH".
- Evidence: rules that run to the sheet edge in `HPnv8y6Wg` and `HTfc8OCW`. Mark on the horizon in `HIBHih_W` and `HD66vVFX`. Guides brighter than the logo in `HRYlrT6X`.

## 7. Three things to stop doing

1. **Stop drawing data that is not data.** Procedural scribbles, bars standing in for telemetry, and three different placeholder stations undercut a benchmark brand more than any colour choice. If a real number or a real trajectory is not available, leave the space empty and say so.
2. **Stop faking materials in CSS.** Gradient metal, embossed text, soft drop shadows and tilted cards look like a template pack. Draw objects as line art, or do not show them.
3. **Stop using one treatment everywhere.** The two-tone ExtraBold headline with a violet box, the same noise overlay on every sheet, and 9.5 px low-contrast captions that carry the explanation. Add the blue of p02 to this list: the parent brand has no hue.
